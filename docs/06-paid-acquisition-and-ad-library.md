# Paid Acquisition and Ad Library

## Launch objective

Use paid traffic to answer one question first:

**Will a stranger who is actively trying to figure out a tattoo pay $99 to talk it through with Joker?**

Do not optimize for email leads. Optimize for paid bookings.

## Initial budget

Start with a tightly capped ChatGPT Ads test.

- Daily budget: platform minimum or $25/day, whichever is required.
- Initial hard spend cap: $200–$250.
- Geography: United States.
- Device: all eligible devices unless performance data later supports narrowing.
- Objective: CPC initially if purchase-event volume is too low for conversion optimization.
- Primary conversion: completed paid booking.
- Secondary analytics: pricing section view, scheduler open, checkout start, intake completion.

The $47 tier is not the acquisition target. The page can sell it, but paid creative should primarily sell the underlying problem and the $99 outcome.

## Intent cluster 1 — “I want a tattoo but I don’t know what I want”

### Context / targeting themes
- wants a meaningful tattoo but does not know the imagery;
- asking for tattoo ideas based on a person, memory, experience, value, or story;
- has a vague tattoo idea and wants help developing it;
- comparing tattoo symbolism;
- trying to make a tattoo personal rather than generic;
- asking ChatGPT to brainstorm a tattoo concept.

### Ad concept A — Blank-page problem
**Title:** Know You Want a Tattoo?
**Body:** Talk it through with an experienced tattoo artist and turn the idea into a clear plan.

Visual: Joker at a table/video screen reviewing a few references, not a finished tattoo.

### Ad concept B — Meaning without imagery
**Title:** Know What It Means?
**Body:** If the story is clear but the tattoo isn’t, Joker can help you work out the direction.

Visual: simple split of notes/story on one side and organized brief on the other.

### Ad concept C — Anti-generator
**Title:** Don’t Need Another AI Tattoo
**Body:** You may need a tattooer who knows which questions to ask before your artist designs it.

Use only if platform review and tone allow. The page must reinforce that this is human consultation, not anti-AI fearmongering.

## Intent cluster 2 — “I can’t explain it to my artist”

### Context / targeting themes
- preparing for a tattoo consultation;
- nervous about talking to a tattoo artist;
- asking how to explain tattoo references;
- worried the artist will misunderstand;
- asking what to bring to a consultation;
- asking how much creative freedom to give an artist.

### Ad concept A — Communication
**Title:** Explain Your Tattoo Idea
**Body:** Get clear on what matters, what can change, and what your artist needs to understand.

Visual: one-page Artist Brief with a few highlighted sections.

### Ad concept B — Shy client
**Title:** Nervous About the Consult?
**Body:** Work through the idea first with Joker, then walk into your artist’s consultation prepared.

Visual: Joker on video call; client side implied rather than showing anxiety theatrically.

### Ad concept C — Thirty screenshots
**Title:** 30 Screenshots, No Plan?
**Body:** Joker helps you find the common thread and turn references into useful direction.

Visual: messy reference spread becoming three concise reference notes.

## Intent cluster 3 — Large-project planning

### Context / targeting themes
- how to plan a tattoo sleeve;
- how to make a sleeve cohesive;
- what to include in a memorial sleeve;
- planning a backpiece / full leg / large cover-up;
- combining multiple subjects in one tattoo;
- preserving future tattoo space.

### Ad concept A — Sleeve hierarchy
**Title:** Planning a Full Sleeve?
**Body:** Sort the focal points, supporting ideas and body flow before the design starts.

Visual: blank arm photo with broad project zones/notes, explicitly not a tattoo design.

### Ad concept B — Too many elements
**Title:** Too Much for One Tattoo?
**Body:** Figure out what belongs, what supports it, and what should be left out.

Visual: list of many elements narrowed to priority 1/2/3.

### Ad concept C — Cohesion
**Title:** Make the Project Cohesive
**Body:** Plan one tattoo project instead of handing your artist a pile of disconnected ideas.

# Organic creative library

Paid ads should be supported by organic proof/content.

## Short-form video concepts

### 1. “The client said yes, but I knew they meant maybe.”
Hook:
> “One of the most important things I learned as a tattooer is that clients will sometimes agree with you because they’re nervous.”

Teach how an experienced artist notices hesitation and invites disagreement. CTA: Tattoo Blueprint.

### 2. “Stop describing your references like this.”
Show: “I want this tattoo, but different.”
Then teach: describe what you like — contrast, mood, pose, scale, composition — instead of asking another artist to copy it.

### 3. “You don’t need a tattoo idea yet.”
Explain that sometimes the right first question is what the tattoo needs to mean, not what object it contains.

### 4. “Your sleeve does not need 14 symbols.”
Explain hierarchy and why meaning can survive without literal representation of every story point.

### 5. “What to tell your tattoo artist vs what to let them decide.”
Client owns intent/preferences/boundaries; executing artist owns design/tattooability/composition/technical execution.

### 6. Case study format
“Client came in saying X. The real issue turned out to be Y. We removed Z. Here is how the executing artist interpreted the final direction.”

Credit artist prominently.

# Landing-page message match

Each ad should land on the same core page initially, but the hero can later be dynamically matched by UTM intent.

- unclear idea ad → hero language about figuring out what it should be;
- communication ad → hero language about walking into the artist consultation prepared;
- sleeve ad → hero language about planning a cohesive large project.

Do not build three pages until one page has enough traffic to show where the friction is.

# Performance thresholds

These are internal operating rules, not industry benchmarks.

## Delivery check
After first $25–$50:
- Are there impressions?
- Are clicks contextually relevant?
- Are people reaching pricing?

If not, fix targeting/ad delivery before judging the offer.

## Early signal
Within first $100:
- one paid customer = meaningful signal;
- zero purchases does not kill the idea, but inspect recordings/analytics/message match.

## Initial validation
By $250 paid spend:
- 3+ paid customers = continue;
- 1–2 customers = diagnose page/creative/tier mix before scaling;
- 0 customers after ~50 relevant clicks = pause and materially revise.

## CAC guardrails
At founding prices:
- <$35 blended CAC: strong enough to scale carefully;
- $35–$50: workable while learning if $99/$250 dominate;
- $50–$75: only tolerable if repeat/referral behavior begins proving LTV;
- >$75: do not scale the current funnel.

## Tier mix target
Desired initial mix:
- $47: 15–25%
- $99: 60–70%
- $250: 10–20%

If $47 exceeds ~35% of paid traffic sales, narrow its scope and/or stop emphasizing “starting at $47” in ads.

# Creative test matrix

Run no more than two creatives per intent cluster initially.

Track:
- ad ID;
- intent cluster;
- title/body;
- visual concept;
- spend;
- clicks;
- landing sessions;
- paid orders;
- tier ordered;
- CAC;
- revenue;
- consultation show rate;
- qualitative fit.

Do not crown winners on CTR alone. A lower-CTR ad that generates $99/$250 customers is more valuable than bargain-curiosity clicks.

---

# Retargeting / later channels

Do not launch these until core funnel works:
- Meta retargeting for site visitors;
- Instagram Reels organic-to-paid winners;
- Google Search for explicit “tattoo consultation / tattoo planning” queries;
- artist referral QR/link;
- client referral links;
- email follow-up for abandoned paid booking only if email was voluntarily captured.

Do not build the initial plan around broad tattoo-interest Meta traffic. Start with high-intent conversations and proof-driven organic content.
