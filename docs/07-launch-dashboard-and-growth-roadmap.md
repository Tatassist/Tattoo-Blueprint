# First 10-client review

Do not change the entire product after every call. After 10 completed paid calls, review:

1. Which intake questions produced useful information?
2. Which questions clients repeatedly misunderstood?
3. Which live questions produced breakthroughs?
4. Which Blueprint sections artists/clients actually referenced?
5. Which sections were decorative or redundant?
6. Average founder time per tier.
7. Which tier buyers should have chosen instead.
8. What clients expected that was not included.
9. Whether “Tattoo Blueprint” is understood as planning rather than design.
10. Whether the one-page artist brief feels respectful to artists.

Delete low-value steps before adding new ones.

# First 25-client decision gates

## Keep $47 if:
- it converts people who would not buy $99;
- it stays within 30 founder minutes;
- it creates upgrades/referrals;
- it does not dominate paid-acquisition sales.

## Modify or hide $47 from paid traffic if:
- >35% of paid buyers select it;
- average CAC approaches the order price;
- clients repeatedly bring full-project expectations into the short call.

## Keep $99 unchanged if:
- it is 55–75% of sales;
- average founder time <= 60 minutes;
- clarity delta is strong;
- artists/clients use the brief.

## Test $119 with identical scope if:
- $99 time slots are filling;
- paid CAC is comfortably below target;
- demand appears insensitive to price;
- no additional deliverables are required.

Run price test against $99; do not simply raise permanently by assumption.

## Keep $250 if:
- major-project clients genuinely need the deeper call;
- it prevents complex projects from clogging the $99 tier;
- clients perceive value without expecting design work.

# Growth phases

## Phase 1 — Paid client service
0–25 clients.

Only goal: prove the conversation is worth paying for.

Assets created naturally:
- testimonials;
- real client language;
- Blueprint examples;
- artist reactions;
- finished projects;
- content topics.

## Phase 2 — Relationship flywheel
25–75 clients.

Focus:
- personal client follow-up;
- repeat projects;
- client referrals;
- artist feedback;
- case studies;
- identifying artists who appreciate the process.

Do not build an artist directory.

## Phase 3 — Artist referral lane
Start when at least 5 artists independently say the brief helped or indicate they would send clients.

Offer:
> “If you have a client who has a meaningful idea but cannot organize it, send them here. They pay us directly and come back to you with a concise brief. You keep design and execution control.”

Provide artists:
- unique referral link;
- explanation of what the client receives;
- sample Artist Brief;
- explicit statement that no referral payment is required.

Measure:
- referrals per artist;
- conversion rate of artist-referred clients;
- repeat referrals;
- artist satisfaction.

## Phase 4 — B2B paid pilot
Only after unpaid referral lane proves demand.

Test one model at a time.

### Model A — Studio-sponsored Blueprint
Studio pays for the consultation as part of its premium client process.

Candidate test pricing:
- single normal Blueprint: $99 retail-equivalent initially;
- five prepaid consultations: test a modest volume discount only after real demand;
- executing artist receives the brief and can request format changes.

Do not invent a huge wholesale package before an artist asks for it.

### Model B — Consultation overflow
Artist sends specific difficult/unclear leads. Client or artist pays depending on relationship.

### Model C — TatAssist DIY path
If the artist says, “I want to learn how to do this myself,” TatAssist becomes the relevant product rather than forcing outsourced consultation.

## Phase 5 — Consultant delegation
Trigger only when:
- demand exceeds Joker's desired consulting hours;
- call SOP is repeatable;
- quality can be scored;
- at least 100 calls provide training examples.

Candidate consultants:
- experienced tattooers reducing chair time;
- veteran shop owners;
- tattoo professionals with unusually strong communication skills.

Quality test:
- mock consult;
- recognize shy agreement;
- establish hierarchy;
- distinguish meaning from imagery;
- leave execution freedom to artist;
- produce concise, non-generic briefs.

Keep a premium Joker-led tier only if market demand supports it.

## Phase 6 — Recurring collector access
Trigger only after repeated requests for structured continuing access.

Test with invite-only cohort.

Candidate economics:
- $24/month;
- ~$230/year (about 20% off monthly total);
- bounded benefits;
- normal human follow-up remains free.

## Phase 7 — Artist matching / network
Trigger only when the private artist relationship graph is useful enough that Joker can recommend from real knowledge, not scraped portfolios.

Possible future client service:
- paid shortlist/research;
- geography/style/project-fit criteria;
- transparent disclosure of any commercial relationship with recommended artist.

Never let an artist secretly pay for ranking.

## Phase 8 — Broader industry layer
Potential later extensions if the core relationship network becomes substantial:
- artist case-study media;
- client education content;
- TatAssist cross-sell;
- supplier/aftercare sponsorships with disclosure;
- tattoo travel planning partnerships;
- conventions/events as relationship channels, not the business foundation;
- industry reports based on anonymized client/artist process data;
- curated artist network;
- tools/software only when a repeated manual bottleneck is proven.

The unifying business is built from repeated useful relationships, not from announcing a platform.

# What not to build before 100 clients

- custom SaaS platform;
- public artist marketplace;
- ranking algorithm;
- escrow/payments between artist and client;
- certification program;
- giant affiliate store;
- tattoo generator;
- mobile app;
- complicated subscription;
- automated “AI consultant” that removes Joker from the core $99 experience.

---

# Launch definition of done

Tattoo Blueprint is ready for paid traffic when a stranger can:
1. understand the service in under 30 seconds;
2. see the three prices;
3. know that $99 is the normal choice;
4. book and pay without contacting staff;
5. complete the correct intake;
6. attend the consultation;
7. receive a concise artifact within 24 hours;
8. understand that their artist still designs the tattoo;
9. enter a functioning follow-up pipeline;
10. be attributed back to the exact acquisition source.
