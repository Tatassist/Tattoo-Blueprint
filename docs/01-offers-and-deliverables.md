# Offers and Exact Deliverables

## 1. Tattoo Clarity Call — $47

### Who it is for
A client with one stuck point, not an entire complex project.

Examples:
- “I know what this tattoo means but not what imagery fits.”
- “I have two directions and cannot decide.”
- “I need help figuring out placement.”
- “I want a memorial tattoo without the obvious clichés.”
- “I have an idea but I cannot tell whether it is too much for one tattoo.”

### Exact client experience
1. Purchase and book a 20-minute video call.
2. Complete the short Clarity Intake.
3. Joker reads the intake immediately before the call.
4. 20-minute focused consultation.
5. Client receives a concise Clarity Recap within 24 hours.

### Exact deliverable: Clarity Recap
One page maximum.

Sections:
- **Your Core Idea:** 2–4 sentences describing the idea in plain language.
- **What Matters Most:** 1–3 bullets.
- **Recommended Direction:** Joker's concise recommendation.
- **Keep Flexible:** what the executing artist should be allowed to solve.
- **Next Step:** what to do before or during the artist consultation.

### Not included
- full project architecture;
- reference board;
- artist shortlist;
- design review;
- follow-up call;
- final tattoo design;
- multiple unrelated tattoo ideas.

### Scope control language
If the client brings a project that clearly needs a full Blueprint:
> “There are enough moving parts here that I don’t want to give you a shallow answer in a 20-minute call. We can use today to identify the main issue, and if you want the full project mapped out afterward I’ll credit the $47 toward a Tattoo Blueprint within seven days.”

This creates a natural upgrade without making the $47 tier fake.

## 2. Tattoo Blueprint — $99

### Who it is for
The normal client with one complete tattoo project who wants to understand and communicate it clearly.

Examples:
- forearm/upper-arm custom piece;
- memorial tattoo;
- medium-to-large black and grey or color concept;
- a meaningful piece with several possible symbols;
- first substantial tattoo;
- client already has an artist but struggles to articulate the concept.

### Exact client experience
1. Purchase and book a 45-minute call.
2. Complete full Tattoo Blueprint Intake.
3. Joker reviews intake and references for approximately 5 minutes before the call.
4. 40–45-minute consultation.
5. AI organizes transcript/notes into the template.
6. Joker reviews, edits, and approves.
7. Client receives two files/sections within 24 hours:
   - Client Tattoo Blueprint;
   - Artist Brief.

### Exact deliverable A: Client Tattoo Blueprint
Target length: 1–2 pages.

Sections:
1. **The Tattoo in One Sentence**
2. **What It Represents**
3. **Primary Visual Direction**
4. **Supporting Elements**
5. **Visual Priority / Focal Point**
6. **Placement and Approximate Scale**
7. **Style / Mood Direction**
8. **Must Keep**
9. **Can Change**
10. **Avoid**
11. **Reference Notes**
12. **Questions to Take to the Artist**

### Exact deliverable B: Artist Brief
One page maximum. Designed to be handed directly to the executing tattoo artist.

Sections:
- project summary;
- intended placement/scale;
- primary subject;
- supporting ideas;
- emotional/style direction;
- non-negotiables;
- areas of creative freedom;
- things to avoid;
- selected reference notes;
- unresolved questions for the artist.

Footer:
> This brief is meant to reduce ambiguity, not dictate the final artwork. Final composition, tattooability, design, and execution remain with the tattoo artist.

### Not included
- finished design;
- stencil;
- unlimited revisions;
- artist management;
- ongoing text access;
- tattoo price estimate unless the conversation naturally permits a broad educational range and it is clearly framed as non-binding;
- guarantee that the executing artist agrees with Joker's suggestions.

## 3. Major Project Blueprint — $250

### Who it is for
Projects where composition across a large body area is itself a major planning problem.

Examples:
- full sleeve;
- half sleeve with multiple focal subjects;
- backpiece;
- chest panel;
- full leg project;
- large cover-up/rework planning;
- multiple connected tattoo concepts;
- client trying to preserve future space while planning current work.

### Exact client experience
1. Purchase and book a 90-minute session.
2. Complete Major Project Intake.
3. Submit full body-area photos and references.
4. Joker reviews intake for up to 10 minutes before the call.
5. 75–90-minute project consultation.
6. AI organizes notes.
7. Joker reviews and finalizes.
8. Client receives:
   - Major Project Blueprint;
   - one-page Artist Brief.

### Exact deliverable A: Major Project Blueprint
Target length: 3–5 pages. No decorative filler.

Sections:
1. **Project Intent**
2. **Overall Story / Theme**
3. **Primary Focal Subjects**
4. **Secondary / Connecting Elements**
5. **Priority Hierarchy**
6. **Body-Area Map in Words**
7. **Scale and Flow Considerations**
8. **Style / Contrast / Mood**
9. **Existing Tattoos / Constraints**
10. **Must Keep**
11. **Can Change**
12. **Remove / Avoid**
13. **Future-Proofing Considerations**
14. **Reference Notes**
15. **Artist Fit Criteria**
16. **Questions for the Artist**
17. **Likely Decisions the Artist Must Make**

### Exact deliverable B: Artist Brief
Still one page. The artist should not have to read the long client document.

### Not included
- artist shortlist as a default deliverable;
- artist booking or negotiation;
- final design;
- session-by-session drawing instructions;
- unlimited support;
- months of check-ins.

# Upgrade rules

## $47 → $99
If the client upgrades within 7 days, credit the full $47. They pay $52 more.

## $99 → $250
If the consultation reveals that the project is genuinely a major-project planning problem, offer a $151 upgrade only if additional consultation time and expanded planning are actually needed. Never force an upgrade mid-call solely because the tattoo is expensive.

## No automatic upsell
If the $99 service is enough, finish the $99 service.

# Fulfillment standards

Every deliverable must:
- sound like the client, not generic AI copy;
- distinguish meaning from imagery;
- identify hierarchy rather than dumping a list of symbols;
- explicitly state what can remain flexible for the artist;
- avoid pretending Joker has designed the final tattoo;
- avoid telling the executing artist how to technically tattoo;
- preserve uncertainty when uncertainty still exists;
- be concise enough that a working tattoo artist will actually read it.
