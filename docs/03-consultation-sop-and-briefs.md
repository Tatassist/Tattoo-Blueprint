# Consultation SOP and Brief Templates

## Consultation principle

The call is not a questionnaire read aloud. The intake prevents wasting live time on facts. The live call is for interpretation, tension, hesitation, prioritization, and helping the client say what they could not say on the form.

Joker leads the conversation. Do not make a nervous client carry the consult.

## Signals to notice

Pause and probe when the client:
- suddenly becomes less certain;
- agrees too fast;
- says “whatever you think” after previously expressing a preference;
- gives five subjects equal importance;
- keeps returning to one memory or element;
- describes meaning more strongly than imagery;
- says they “just want it to look cool” after telling an emotional story;
- apologizes for not knowing tattoo terminology;
- worries about offending the artist;
- keeps adding elements because they fear leaving meaning out.

Useful interventions:
- “You hesitated there. What part did not feel right?”
- “Forget tattoos for a second. What are you actually trying to remember or say?”
- “If I took that symbol away, would the meaning disappear?”
- “If only one thing could read clearly, what has to win?”
- “Do you want strangers to understand this, or do you want *you* to understand it?”
- “You can tell me no. I am testing directions, not selling you one.”
- “Are you agreeing because you like that, or because you are not sure how to push back?”
- “Which reference feels closest, and what is it doing that the others are not?”
- “What would make you see the finished tattoo and think, ‘that missed the point’?”

# $47 Clarity Call SOP — 20 minutes

## Minute 0–2: Define the problem
“By the end of this call, what is the one thing you want to feel clearer about?”

Restate it in one sentence and get agreement.

## Minute 2–7: Find the reason underneath the request
Ask only relevant questions:
- Why this tattoo?
- Why now?
- What part matters most?
- Is meaning or appearance driving the decision?

## Minute 7–14: Explore the decision
Compare 2–3 plausible directions. Explain tradeoffs in plain language.

## Minute 14–18: Make the call
Give a recommendation. Do not hide behind “it depends” if enough information exists.

## Minute 18–20: Confirm
“Tell me what you are taking away from this.”

Correct anything that was misunderstood.

# $99 Tattoo Blueprint SOP — 45 minutes

## 0–5: Set the frame
- Confirm the exact project.
- Ask what they most need help articulating.
- Tell them they do not need to protect your feelings or agree with suggestions.

Suggested opener:
> “I read through what you sent. You already gave me the facts, so I do not want to make you repeat a form. I want to figure out what is underneath the idea, what actually has to survive, and what your artist should have freedom to solve.”

## 5–15: Story before symbols
Questions:
- Why does this tattoo exist?
- What memory/person/change/idea is underneath it?
- What part of the story hits hardest?
- What should the tattoo make *you* feel?
- Does anyone else need to understand it?

Do not suggest imagery too early.

## 15–25: Translate meaning into visual priorities
- What images are already attached to the story naturally?
- Which symbols are meaningful versus decorative?
- Which references contain qualities the client actually wants?
- What is the primary subject?
- What can support without competing?
- What should be omitted?

Force hierarchy if needed:
> “Rank these. One is the star, two supports it, three can disappear if the artist needs room.”

## 25–33: Body and scale
- Review placement photos.
- Discuss approximate scale.
- Existing work/constraints.
- Whether the concept needs more room.
- Whether body flow changes the composition idea.

Stay at planning level. Do not dictate another artist's technical execution.

## 33–39: Artist handoff
- What must the artist understand?
- What should the client leave open?
- What are they nervous to say?
- What questions should they ask?

## 39–43: Reflect the project back
Summarize:
1. what it means;
2. what the visual priority is;
3. what is flexible;
4. what is explicitly unwanted.

Ask: “Does that sound like *your* tattoo?”

## 43–45: Close
Explain what the written brief will contain and remind them the executing artist designs the tattoo.

# $250 Major Project SOP — 90 minutes

## 0–10: Define the project boundary
- Exact body area.
- One project versus connected projects.
- Existing tattoos and future space.
- What success means.

## 10–30: Story architecture
Identify themes and separate them from the client's inventory of symbols.

Create three internal buckets:
- essential meaning;
- useful visual material;
- optional decoration.

## 30–50: Hierarchy and visual architecture
- primary focal subject(s);
- supporting subjects;
- transitions/atmosphere;
- areas that need breathing room;
- what should read from distance versus close;
- what can be eliminated.

## 50–65: Body map
Talk through zones in plain language, e.g. outer upper arm, inner upper arm, elbow transition, outer forearm. Do not pretend to finalize a composition that belongs to the executing artist.

## 65–75: Artist fit and feasibility questions
What portfolio evidence should the client look for? What should they ask in consultation? What aspects require specialist experience?

## 75–85: Stress test
Ask:
- “If the artist tells you one element needs to go, which one goes?”
- “If this needs to be 20% larger, are you still excited about it?”
- “What part would you regret compromising?”
- “What part are you over-attached to because you have been staring at references?”

## 85–90: Reflect and confirm
Summarize the architecture and unresolved decisions.

# AI drafting prompt — internal

Use the transcript and intake to draft the brief. Do not invent preferences, symbolism, or certainty.

Prompt:

“Create a concise Tattoo Blueprint from the supplied client intake and consultation transcript. Preserve the client's own terminology and emotional framing. Separate what the client explicitly said from Joker's recommendations. Identify primary visual priority, supporting elements, must-keep items, flexible items, avoid items, placement/scale considerations, reference-specific notes, and unresolved questions. Do not create a finished tattoo design. Do not add generic symbolism the client did not discuss. Do not make technical claims for the executing artist. If two statements conflict, flag the conflict rather than silently reconciling it. Produce (1) a 1–2 page client Blueprint and (2) a one-page artist brief.”
