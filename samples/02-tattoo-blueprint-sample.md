# Fictional Sample - $99 Tattoo Blueprint

> This is a fictional demonstration created for Tattoo Blueprint. It is not a real client record.

## What the client submitted

**Client:** Daniel M.  
**Placement:** outer upper arm / shoulder area  
**Approximate size:** 7-10 inches  
**Artist status:** researching artists

> “I want something for my dad. He passed a few years ago and I have put this off because every idea starts feeling corny. We fished a lot, he rode motorcycles, listened to Johnny Cash nonstop, and he had this old pocket watch from his grandfather. I have saved tattoos with clocks, fishing poles, old motorcycles, roses, lake scenes, portraits, and song lyrics. I do not necessarily need all of it but I do not know what to cut. I definitely do not want one of those memorial tattoos that looks like a collage of random objects. I want it masculine and kind of quiet. He was not an emotional guy. The fishing memories are probably the strongest because that is when we actually talked.”

### Reference notes from intake

- Lake scene reference: likes the open negative space and quiet mood, not the exact landscape.
- Pocket watch reference: likes the age/detail but is unsure whether the watch matters enough to be a focal point.
- Motorcycle reference: likes the black-and-grey realism, but admits motorcycles are more associated with his dad publicly than personally.
- Johnny Cash portrait reference: likes the dark contrast and mood, does not want a celebrity portrait.
- Fishing lure reference: likes that it can be specific to his dad without explaining itself to everyone.

### What emerged in the call

- The tattoo is really about the **private version of his father**, not a summary of every recognizable hobby.
- Fishing is the strongest memory because it represents time together and conversation.
- The client does not need strangers to understand that it is a memorial.
- The motorcycle and pocket watch became less important once he ranked the memories.
- Johnny Cash is useful as a tonal reference: restrained, dark, unsentimental, not literal imagery.
- The client wants the piece to feel like a memory rather than a shrine.
- He prefers one strong focal idea with atmospheric support over several equally detailed objects.

# Daniel M. - Tattoo Blueprint

**Consultation date:** Fictional sample  
**Project:** Memorial tattoo for father  
**Planned placement:** Outer upper arm / shoulder  
**Approximate scale:** Medium-large, roughly 7-10 inches depending on artist recommendation

## The tattoo in one sentence

A quiet black-and-grey memorial built around the fishing memories where Daniel felt closest to his father, with enough restraint that it reads as a strong tattoo first and a personal memorial second.

## What it represents

The tattoo is not meant to catalog Daniel's father's hobbies. It represents the time they spent together when conversation came naturally and Daniel got access to a side of his father that other people did not always see. Fishing is the clearest doorway into that memory.

The emotional target is **quiet closeness**, not grief, tragedy, or overt sentimentality.

## Primary visual direction

Build the project around **one fishing-related focal subject or moment** that can carry the memory without requiring explanation.

The strongest direction is not necessarily a full scenic illustration. Daniel is open to the artist deciding whether the focal subject is an object, a cropped moment, or a more atmospheric fishing image, as long as the result feels specific and grounded rather than generic “outdoors” imagery.

The visual should allow the artist to use black-and-grey contrast and atmosphere to create the restrained mood Daniel responded to in the Johnny Cash references without including Johnny Cash himself.

## Supporting elements

Potential support, only if the artist believes it strengthens the composition:

- restrained lake/water atmosphere
- subtle environmental texture or horizon
- one personally specific fishing detail, such as a lure/tackle reference connected to his father
- negative space that keeps the piece from becoming a dense collage

The pocket watch and motorcycle do **not** need to be included simply because they were associated with his father.

## Visual priority

1. **Fishing / shared time together** - the emotional center.
2. **Quiet atmosphere** - establishes the memory and tone.
3. **One personal detail** - optional, only if it adds specificity without competing.

Anything beyond those priorities should have to earn its place.

## Placement and scale

The outer upper arm/shoulder remains a good starting area because it gives the artist enough surface to create depth and atmosphere without forcing a long vertical composition.

Daniel should show the artist the entire upper arm and shoulder rather than choosing an exact rectangle in advance. If the artist recommends wrapping slightly toward the front/back of the arm to create better flow, Daniel is open to that.

The piece should not be reduced simply to hit an arbitrary inch measurement. The artist should recommend the smallest scale at which the focal subject and atmospheric separation remain readable.

## Style / mood direction

- black and grey
- realistic or realism-influenced
- strong but controlled contrast
- quiet, masculine, restrained
- atmospheric rather than decorative
- not overly polished or sentimental

Daniel repeatedly responded to images that felt cinematic and still rather than busy.

## Must keep

- the project must remain centered on the fishing relationship
- it should feel personal without needing obvious memorial text
- one clear focal idea, not a pile of equal-priority symbols
- the emotional tone should stay restrained

## Can change

- exact fishing subject
- whether the composition is scenic or object-focused
- exact perspective and orientation
- amount of environmental atmosphere
- whether one personal fishing detail is used
- exact wrap/placement on the upper arm

## Avoid

- clock + rose + motorcycle + fishing pole collage
- RIP lettering, dates, halos, wings, or obvious memorial framing unless Daniel later specifically chooses them
- song lyrics added just to reference Johnny Cash
- a celebrity portrait
- generic wilderness imagery that loses the personal fishing connection
- excessive micro-detail that forces the piece too small or visually crowded

## Reference notes

**Lake scene reference**  
Useful for: openness, negative space, quiet atmosphere.  
Not a request to copy: exact shoreline, mountains, or composition.

**Pocket watch reference**  
Useful for: aged texture and realism detail.  
Current decision: not important enough to require inclusion.

**Motorcycle reference**  
Useful for: black-and-grey treatment and contrast.  
Current decision: motorcycle imagery is not central to the memory being represented.

**Johnny Cash reference**  
Useful for: dark, restrained, unsentimental mood.  
Not a request for: portrait, lyrics, logo, or literal Cash imagery.

**Fishing lure reference**  
Useful for: the idea of a specific object that could feel personal without explaining itself publicly.  
Artist should decide whether this helps or distracts from the main focal direction.

## Questions to take to the artist

1. If you were building this around the fishing memory rather than a list of symbols, what would you make the focal point?
2. How much upper-arm space would you want for the idea to read clearly?
3. Would you treat it as a contained piece or let it wrap slightly for better flow?
4. Is one personal fishing detail enough to make it specific, or would it clutter the main image?
5. Looking at your portfolio, which pieces best show the kind of restrained black-and-grey atmosphere this project needs?

## Final reminder

Your artist should still design the tattoo. This Blueprint exists so you can communicate the right intention and priorities while leaving the artist room to solve the final composition.

# One-Page Artist Intent Brief

## Tattoo Intent Brief - Daniel M.

**Placement:** Outer upper arm / shoulder  
**Approximate scale:** 7-10 inches as a starting expectation; open to artist recommendation  
**Project type:** Black-and-grey memorial

### Core intent

Daniel wants a memorial for his father centered on the private fishing memories where they spent meaningful time together. The goal is not to summarize his father's hobbies or create an obvious memorial collage. He wants the tattoo to feel quiet, masculine, personal, and restrained.

### Primary subject

Fishing-related focal imagery or moment. Daniel is intentionally leaving the exact subject/perspective open so the artist can find the strongest visual solution.

### Supporting ideas

- subtle lake/water atmosphere if useful
- negative space / breathing room
- possibly one personally specific fishing detail such as a lure or tackle reference

### Direction

Black-and-grey, realism or realism-influenced, controlled contrast, cinematic/still mood. Strong tattoo first; memorial meaning can remain private.

### Non-negotiables

- fishing relationship remains the emotional center
- one clear hierarchy rather than many equal symbols
- no requirement that strangers recognize it as a memorial
- restrained rather than sentimental tone

### Creative freedom

- exact focal fishing subject
- scenic vs object-focused treatment
- crop/perspective/orientation
- amount of atmosphere
- exact scale and wrap on upper arm
- whether the optional personal detail improves the design

### Avoid

- clock/rose/motorcycle/fishing collage
- RIP framing, wings/halos, or memorial clichés by default
- Johnny Cash portrait or lyrics
- adding hobbies solely because they were associated with his father

### Reference notes

References are being used for mood, contrast, negative space, and specificity - not as designs to copy.

### Open questions for the artist

- What focal treatment best communicates the fishing memory at this placement?
- What scale/wrap gives the image enough room without unnecessary size?
- Would the optional personal lure/tackle detail improve specificity or compete with the focal image?

> This brief is meant to reduce ambiguity, not dictate the final artwork. Final composition, tattooability, design, and execution remain with the tattoo artist.
