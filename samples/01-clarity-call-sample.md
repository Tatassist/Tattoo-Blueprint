# Fictional Sample - $47 Tattoo Clarity Call

> This is a fictional demonstration created for Tattoo Blueprint. It is not a real client record.

## What the client submitted

**Client:** Maya R.  
**Stuck point:** imagery and placement

> “I want something about starting over after a divorce, but I really do not want a phoenix. That feels too obvious for me. I keep saving wildflowers, broken chains, moths, and little doorway/window tattoos. I like the idea of something about freedom but I do not want it to look angry or like a breakup tattoo. I was thinking inner forearm because I want to see it, but I am worried that might make it feel too delicate or small. I just want it to feel like I got my own life back.”

### What emerged in the call

- The client was not trying to memorialize the divorce itself.
- “Freedom” was less important than **self-possession and choosing her own direction again**.
- Broken chains felt too aggressive once discussed aloud.
- The client repeatedly returned to the idea of something growing without being arranged or contained.
- She wanted the tattoo to feel calm and self-assured, not triumphant or dramatic.
- She liked being able to see the tattoo herself, but was open to moving it if the imagery needed more room.

# Tattoo Clarity Recap

## Your core idea

This tattoo is about having your own life back after a period where your choices felt shaped around someone else. It does not need to read as divorce, heartbreak, or “survival.” The stronger direction is quiet independence: something that feels self-directed, alive, and no longer contained.

## What matters most

- The tattoo should feel **calm, independent, and personal**, not angry.
- It should communicate growth without using an obvious rebirth symbol.
- You want to be able to look at it and remember the feeling of choosing your own direction again.

## Recommended direction

Use **uncontained botanical growth** as the visual language rather than chains, a phoenix, or a literal “freedom” symbol. The key is not a specific flower. The idea is that the composition should feel naturally self-directed instead of arranged into a perfect bouquet.

A tattoo artist could interpret that in many ways: one or several wild botanical elements, asymmetrical growth, stems moving with the body, or another organic subject with the same feeling. The artist should still decide the final imagery and composition.

## Placement

The inner forearm still makes sense because seeing the piece yourself is part of why you want it. Do not lock yourself into a tiny version just to keep it there. Show the artist the full area and ask what scale lets the growth feel natural instead of compressed.

If the artist believes the idea needs more movement than the inner forearm can comfortably provide, the outer forearm or upper arm are reasonable alternatives. The emotional reason for wanting visibility should remain part of that conversation.

## Keep flexible

- exact flower species
- number of stems/elements
- precise orientation
- fine-line versus more dimensional execution
- whether one subtle secondary symbol improves the concept

## Avoid

- phoenix imagery
- broken chains
- overt breakup/divorce symbolism
- quotes about freedom or strength
- anything that feels aggressively victorious
- forcing too many separate symbols into one small piece

## Next step

Tell your tattoo artist this is **not a tattoo about the divorce**. It is about the quieter feeling that came afterward: having your own direction again. Ask them how they would visually express uncontained growth on the body area you are considering, then pay attention to whether their interpretation still feels calm and personal to you.
