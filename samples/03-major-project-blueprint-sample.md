# Fictional Sample - $250 Major Project Blueprint

> This is a fictional demonstration created for Tattoo Blueprint. It is not a real client record.

## What the client submitted

**Client:** Alyssa K.  
**Project:** Full left sleeve  
**Style direction:** Color realism / painterly realism  
**Artist status:** Has two artists in mind, not booked  
**Existing tattoos:** Small script near inner wrist that may remain

> “I want a sleeve about the women in my family and I have way too many ideas. My grandmother basically raised me and she grew magnolias. My mom always had hummingbird stuff everywhere and we used to joke about it. My daughter’s birth flower is a marigold. I also have a photo of the house my grandparents lived in, my grandmother’s handwriting from an old birthday card, a locket she gave me, and I thought maybe a clock at 4:17 because that is when my daughter was born. I like realistic flowers and birds but I do not want it to look like a flower sleeve with random sentimental objects stuck in it. I want it feminine but not super delicate. I like saturated color. I want people to notice the sleeve from a distance but still find personal details close up. I am worried I am trying to put the whole family history on one arm.”

### Initial element list

- magnolias for grandmother
- hummingbird for mother
- marigolds for daughter
- grandparents' house
- grandmother's handwriting
- grandmother's locket
- clock set to 4:17
- daughter's initials
- possibly a quote about generations
- small existing wrist script

### What survived the “keep only three” exercise

1. Magnolias - strongest direct memory of grandmother and visually large enough to anchor the sleeve.
2. Hummingbird - immediately feels like her mother and naturally works with florals.
3. Marigolds - represents her daughter and brings a distinct color/shape into the same visual language.

The house mattered emotionally but Alyssa admitted it did not need to be literally pictured. The locket mattered as an object but felt visually separate from the organic direction. The clock, initials, and quote were mostly attempts to make sure no meaning was “left out.”

### What emerged in the call

- The sleeve is about **continuity between three generations of women**, not a family-history scrapbook.
- Alyssa wants the sleeve to read beautifully from across the room, then reveal personal meaning up close.
- Natural subjects already connect the grandmother, mother, and daughter without forcing unrelated objects into the composition.
- The grandmother should remain the emotional anchor because she is the reason Alyssa started imagining the project.
- The mother and daughter should feel equally respected without requiring equal physical size.
- The grandparents' house can influence atmosphere/color memories without becoming literal architecture.
- Grandmother's handwriting may work as one quiet close-up detail if the artist can integrate it without breaking flow.
- The existing wrist script can potentially remain separate rather than forcing the new sleeve to explain or absorb it.
- Alyssa is open to several sessions and trusts the artist to design transitions.

# Alyssa K. - Major Project Blueprint

**Consultation date:** Fictional sample  
**Project:** Full left sleeve  
**Body area:** Shoulder through wrist, with existing small inner-wrist script  
**Overall direction:** Saturated color realism / painterly realism with strong floral and natural movement

## 1. Project intent

Create one cohesive full-sleeve project about continuity across three generations of women in Alyssa's family. The sleeve should feel lush, confident, and visibly intentional from a distance, while carrying personal references that do not need to be obvious to strangers.

The project should avoid becoming a collection of memorial objects. The strongest common visual language is **living natural imagery**, with each generation represented through a subject already connected to that person.

## 2. Overall story / theme

The sleeve is not “grandmother + mother + daughter” as three separate mini-tattoos. It is about the line connecting them.

Alyssa describes her grandmother as the person who created stability, her mother as energetic and constantly present, and her daughter as the next generation she now gets to nurture. The design should allow those relationships to coexist rather than dividing the arm into three isolated memorial zones.

The emotional tone is **continuity, warmth, and strength**, not grief.

## 3. Primary focal subjects

### Primary anchor: Magnolias / grandmother

The magnolias are the clearest emotional and visual anchor. They can support larger scale, rich realism, and broad forms that read from a distance. The artist should decide exactly where the major bloom(s) sit based on body flow.

### Secondary focal subject: Hummingbird / mother

The hummingbird gives the artist movement and a distinct recognizable focal form without requiring a separate framed scene. It should interact with the larger organic composition rather than appearing pasted onto a floral background.

### Supporting generational subject: Marigolds / daughter

Marigolds connect naturally to the floral language while bringing their own color, texture, and personal meaning. They do not need to occupy the same visual scale as the magnolias to carry equal emotional importance.

## 4. Secondary / connecting elements

Possible, not mandatory:

- leaves/stems/petals used as actual compositional movement rather than filler
- painterly atmosphere/background color that connects the large subjects
- selective light/negative space so the sleeve does not become one solid block of saturated color
- one small piece of grandmother's handwriting if it can live naturally in a quieter area
- visual color cues inspired by memories of the grandparents' home without depicting the house itself

## 5. Priority hierarchy

1. **The sleeve must read as one cohesive piece from a distance.**
2. **Magnolia forms anchor the project emotionally and visually.**
3. **Hummingbird creates movement and a distinct secondary focal moment.**
4. **Marigolds carry the daughter connection and support the larger visual system.**
5. **Personal micro-details are optional rewards for close viewing, not separate focal points.**

If a small symbolic element harms the first four priorities, it should be removed.

## 6. Body-area map in words

This is not a final composition. It defines planning zones and priorities for the artist.

### Shoulder / upper outer arm

Best candidate for one of the largest visual reads. Alyssa wants this area to establish the sleeve immediately when viewed from the side/front. A large magnolia grouping or another broad anchor treatment would make sense here, but exact composition belongs to the artist.

### Upper inner arm

Can carry supporting floral movement, quieter detail, or part of the transition rather than being forced to hold another major “story.” This could be a useful place for a more private detail if the artist sees a natural opportunity.

### Elbow / transition zone

Treat primarily as a flow and durability problem for the artist. Do not force a sentimental object into the elbow simply because the space exists. Botanical forms, atmosphere, or structural movement may be more useful.

### Outer forearm

Needs a strong readable moment so the sleeve does not feel top-heavy. The hummingbird could potentially contribute here or move through this zone depending on the artist's composition, but Alyssa is not prescribing its exact location.

### Inner forearm / wrist

More intimate viewing area. Potential location for subtle handwriting or smaller marigold detail if it fits. Existing wrist script may remain independent. The sleeve does not need to swallow it unless the artist believes integration is visually cleaner.

## 7. Scale and flow considerations

- Use the entire arm as one composition rather than designing individual tattoos to fill each anatomical zone.
- Preserve enough breathing room that individual flowers and the hummingbird remain readable.
- Avoid making every sentimental reference the same size.
- Alyssa is open to wrapping focal forms across front/side/back planes if it improves movement.
- Saturated color is desired, but value contrast and negative space should still create separation.
- Do not reduce the major subjects into small detail just to fit more symbols.

## 8. Style / contrast / mood

Desired qualities:

- color realism or painterly realism
- saturated but intentional color
- confident, substantial forms rather than ultra-delicate fine line
- natural movement around the arm
- depth and contrast that remain readable from several feet away
- painterly transitions are welcome if they keep the sleeve unified
- feminine through subject/movement, not through making everything tiny or fragile

## 9. Existing tattoos / constraints

Alyssa has a small script tattoo near the inner wrist. She is not emotionally attached to integrating it into the new story, but she also does not require it to be covered.

Artist should evaluate whether:

- leaving it visually separate is cleanest;
- surrounding movement can coexist without awkwardly framing it;
- a later cover/integration would improve the sleeve.

No decision needs to be made before the artist sees the arm in person.

## 10. Must keep

- three-generation concept
- magnolia connection to grandmother
- hummingbird connection to mother
- marigold connection to daughter
- cohesive full-sleeve read
- strong color presence
- enough visual hierarchy to prevent scrapbook effect

## 11. Can change

- exact number/species arrangement of flowers beyond the meaningful magnolia/marigold requirement
- exact location of each focal subject
- hummingbird pose/direction
- amount/type of background atmosphere
- whether handwriting is included
- how the existing wrist script is treated
- exact palette within the requested saturated color direction
- number of sessions and technical sequencing

## 12. Remove / avoid

The following elements are **not currently required**:

- literal grandparents' house
- locket
- clock set to 4:17
- daughter's initials
- generation quote

They are meaningful memories, but including all of them would pull the project toward an object collage and weaken the organic visual system.

Avoid:

- three clearly separated “sections,” one per family member
- framed portrait/scene boxes
- tiny symbolic objects floating between flowers
- using background filler only to close gaps after the fact
- sacrificing scale/readability to fit every sentimental reference
- overly pale/delicate treatment that conflicts with Alyssa's preference for strong saturation

## 13. Future-proofing considerations

Alyssa currently views this as a complete left-arm project. She has no immediate plan to extend onto the chest or back, but she should tell the artist if that changes before the shoulder cap is finalized.

If the artist believes the shoulder composition could later transition toward the upper chest/back without damaging the current sleeve, preserving that option is useful but not a launch requirement.

## 14. Reference notes

**Color floral sleeve references**  
Likes: saturation, realistic petal texture, large readable blooms, movement around the arm.  
Avoid: sleeves where every inch is equally dense.

**Hummingbird references**  
Likes: motion, wing blur/painterly movement, natural interaction with flowers.  
Avoid: static “sticker bird” floating on top of a floral sleeve.

**Grandparents' house photo**  
Meaning: place associated with grandmother and childhood stability.  
Current use: emotional/color-memory reference only, not required literal subject matter.

**Handwriting sample**  
Potential use: one subtle personal detail.  
Constraint: should only be included if it integrates without interrupting the sleeve's movement.

## 15. Artist fit criteria

Alyssa should prioritize artists whose healed portfolios show:

- large-scale color realism or painterly realism
- full sleeves designed as one composition
- believable flowers at meaningful scale
- birds/animals integrated into floral compositions
- strong saturation without losing value structure
- transitions around elbow/inner arm that look intentional
- healed color work, not only fresh photographs

Communication matters because Alyssa has many sentimental references and needs an artist comfortable protecting hierarchy rather than agreeing to include everything.

## 16. Questions for the artist

1. Looking at the entire arm, where would you establish the strongest large-scale read?
2. How would you keep the three meaningful subjects connected without making three separate memorial sections?
3. Which areas need intentional breathing room to keep the sleeve readable?
4. Would the handwriting strengthen the composition or feel like a separate element?
5. How would you handle the existing wrist script?
6. Which healed sleeve in your portfolio is closest to the saturation and overall visual strength this project needs?
7. Do any of the three core subjects need to change in scale or placement to work better on the arm?

## 17. Decisions the artist should make

- final composition and exact anatomical placement
- exact magnolia/marigold arrangement
- hummingbird pose and movement
- transitions/background language
- color palette and value structure
- elbow treatment
- integration or separation of existing wrist tattoo
- technical sequencing and session plan

Alyssa should not arrive with these decisions already locked.

## Final reminder

Your artist should still design the sleeve. The Major Project Blueprint defines what the project needs to communicate, the hierarchy that keeps it coherent, and the decisions that matter to you. The artist still owns the final composition, tattooability, and execution.

# One-Page Artist Intent Brief

## Tattoo Intent Brief - Alyssa K.

**Placement:** Full left sleeve, shoulder to wrist  
**Project type:** Three-generation family sleeve  
**Style direction:** Saturated color realism / painterly realism

### Core intent

One cohesive sleeve representing continuity across three generations of women in Alyssa's family. It should read as a strong color sleeve from a distance and reveal personal meaning up close. The goal is continuity and warmth, not a scrapbook of memorial objects.

### Primary subjects

1. Magnolias - grandmother / emotional anchor
2. Hummingbird - mother / movement and secondary focal subject
3. Marigolds - daughter / supporting generational subject

### Direction

Large readable natural forms, strong saturated color, deliberate value contrast, movement around the arm, painterly transitions welcome. Feminine without becoming tiny/delicate.

### Non-negotiables

- all three generational connections remain represented
- one cohesive sleeve read
- meaningful hierarchy
- color remains substantial/saturated
- flowers/bird should feel integrated rather than placed as separate icons

### Creative freedom

- exact focal placement
- number/arrangement of flowers
- hummingbird pose/direction
- background/transitions
- precise color palette
- whether handwriting is useful
- treatment of existing wrist script

### Avoid / currently removed

- literal house
- locket
- 4:17 clock
- daughter's initials
- quote
- three separate memorial panels
- floating small sentimental objects
- making core subjects too small just to include more references

### Optional private detail

Grandmother's handwriting may be included only if it can live naturally in a quieter area without interrupting flow.

### Open questions for the artist

- Where should the largest read live on Alyssa's arm?
- How would you distribute the three subjects without making equal-sized sections?
- Does the handwriting improve or distract?
- How should the existing inner-wrist script be handled?

> This brief is meant to reduce ambiguity, not dictate the final artwork. Final composition, tattooability, design, and execution remain with the tattoo artist.
